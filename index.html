ShopDesk

An AI receptionist that answers a local business's calls, books appointments onto the owner's calendar, and logs every call in a dashboard.

Year
2026
Platform
Web, voice, telephony
History
~300 commits over 6 months
Status
Live at shopdesk.site

The problem

Auto shops, HVAC companies, plumbers and salons lose jobs every day to missed calls. The owner is under a car, on a roof, or with a client. Voicemail doesn't convert, and a human answering service is expensive.

What I built

A product a business owner can set up in a few minutes. ShopDesk reads their website or Google Business Profile, gives them a local phone number, and puts an AI receptionist on it.

The receptionist answers questions, books appointments straight onto the owner's Google Calendar, and transfers urgent calls. Every call, booking and customer shows up in the owner's dashboard.

How a booking works

Caller→Twilio number→Vapi voice agent→Booking service→Google Calendar and database→SMS and email confirmations

Engineering

Structured AI onboarding
The business's site or Google profile is scraped, and Claude turns it into a business profile. A Zod schema validates the output, so a bad model response never reaches the database.
Prompt-injection hardening
Everything an owner types is length-limited and sanitized before it reaches the voice agent's prompt, with unit tests covering it.
Security and multi-tenancy
Webhooks are HMAC-signed, checked in constant time, and fail closed. Row-level security covers every table, and every call, booking and message is tied to the business that owns the assistant.
Scheduling in a live call
The booking service turns phrases like "Thursday at noon" into a real calendar slot while the caller is still on the line.
Quality
TypeScript strict mode, ESLint, Vitest and pytest suites, and GitHub Actions CI on both repositories.

Built with

Next.js, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Supabase (Postgres), Python and FastAPI, Claude API, Vapi, Twilio, Google Calendar API, Vercel and Render.

A recipe app for home cooks and culinary students, planned from a 44-page product plan and built natively for both iPhone and Android.

Year

The problem

Home cooks and culinary students want one place to find recipes, keep their favorites, and follow along while they cook, without losing their place in a long recipe.

What I built

The same product twice, natively on each platform. You can search a library of recipes from the Tasty API, see each recipe's ingredients and instructions, save favorites to your account, and cook with a step-by-step mode. Both apps use Firebase for accounts, profiles and saved recipes.

Planning and design

Before writing code, I wrote a 44-page project plan. It covered the problem and vision, audience research and personas, a competitive analysis of Tasty, Yummly and Kitchen Stories with a SWOT for each, a style guide, wireframes for every screen, flowcharts, and an 8-week sprint plan from proof of concept to release.

Engineering

iOS, in Swift and UIKitStoryboards and tab navigation, custom collection and table view cells, and URLSession networking. Firebase Auth handles login, sign-up and password reset, Firestore stores profiles and saved recipes, and Firebase Storage holds profile photos.
Android, in JavaAndroidX Navigation with fragments for Discover, Details, Step-by-step and Favorites, ViewModels, OkHttp networking and Picasso image loading, on the same Firebase backend.

ResumeMatch

A free AI resume builder and job-match analyzer, with no signup and no paywall.

Year
2026
Platform
Web
AI
Claude API
Status
Live

The problem

Most resume builders charge $25–50 a month for AI features, put PDF downloads behind a paywall, and make you sign up before you can start.

What I built

A guided 7-step wizard with a live resume score, 15 templates, and AI tools that improve bullet points, write summaries and suggest skills.

You can import an existing PDF resume, paste a job description to get a keyword-match analysis, tailor the resume to that job, and generate a matching cover letter. PDF export is free.

Engineering

Serverless AI endpoints
Eight Vercel functions handle analysis, tailoring, rewriting, suggestions, cover letters, and PDF and URL extraction, backed by the Claude API.
Resume import
pdf-parse extracts the text from an uploaded PDF, and Claude maps it into structured fields (experience, education, skills) that fill the editor.
No accounts, no server-side data
Resumes live in the browser's local storage, and the PDF export matches the on-screen preview, page breaks included.

Built with

React, Vite, React Router, Vercel serverless functions, Claude API, jsPDF and pdf-parse.

ShiftSense

An iPhone app that shows service workers what they really earn per hour, with charts and AI insights from their shift history.

Year
2026
Platform
iOS (SwiftUI)
Data
On-device, SwiftData
AI
Claude API

The problem

Servers, bartenders and delivery drivers earn a wage plus tips that change every shift. Most don't know their real hourly rate, or which shifts and workplaces actually pay best.

What I built

An app for logging a shift in seconds: hours, wage, cash and card tips, workplace and tags. A dashboard charts earnings, effective hourly rate, tip mix and trends, and an AI coach turns the history into plain-English insights and progress toward a weekly goal.

Engineering

Local-first data
SwiftData models for shifts, workplaces and settings keep everything on the device. The code is organized by feature, with four Swift Charts views.
Typed AI integration
A service builds a prompt from recent shifts, asks Claude for structured JSON, and decodes it into typed insight models. Errors are typed too, and results are cached so the app doesn't call the API on every view.
Privacy
Nothing leaves the phone unless the user asks for insights, and then only a summary of shifts is sent, using their own API key.

05

Tic-Tac-Toe

A SwiftUI game against a computer opponent that's hard to beat.

Engineering

Built in 2023 with an MVVM structure. The view model owns the game state, detects wins and draws, and chooses the computer's move by priority: win if it can, block the player's winning move, take the center, otherwise a random free square. The view only renders state, and input is disabled while the computer is thinking.
